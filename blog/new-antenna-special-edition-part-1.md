The ISS tracking tool was finally completed.  
Everything was ready—except one crucial thing: I still couldn’t record any audio from the ISS.

On a cold winter day, while staring up at the empty sky in frustration,  
a package arrived at my door.

Inside was something unexpected.

An **ADS-B 1090 MHz dedicated antenna** 🐈️

---

There are several commonly used antennas for ADS-B reception:

- Dipole antenna
- Helical antenna
- Ground plane (GP) antenna
- Collinear antenna

What I chose this time was a **$4 PCB-based compact half-wave dipole antenna**.  
It had a good reputation online, and I had always wanted to try it.

---


![newantenna](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/catgpt-glitch/catgpt-blog-assets/main/posts/202604/newantenna.png)


## Specifications

- ADS-B 1090 MHz antenna
- Gain: 2.0 dBi
- Frequency range: 1060–1120 MHz
- Size: 179 mm × 20 mm (7.05 × 0.79 in)

---

## Installation

- Orientation: **210° (SW)**
- Antenna height: **FL + 70 inches (1788 mm)**
- Mounted indoors, attached to the window frame with repair tape
- Positioned almost identically to the previous V-dipole antenna
