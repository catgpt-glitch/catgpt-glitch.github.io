## 🚀 What is the International Space Station (ISS) ? Part 1

The **International Space Station (ISS)** is a modular space station built and operated as a multinational project.


![iss1](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/catgpt-glitch/catgpt-blog-assets/main/posts/202604/iss1.jpg)


It is a collaboration between five space agencies:

- NASA (United States)
- Roscosmos (Russia)
- JAXA (Japan)
- ESA (Europe)
- CSA (Canada)

The ISS is not just a satellite.  
It functions as a **microgravity laboratory**, supporting research in:

- Space biology
- Astronomy
- Meteorology
- Physics

---

## 🌍 Orbiting Just Above the Earth

The ISS operates in **Low Earth Orbit (LEO)** at an altitude of:

**Approximately 400 km above Earth**

It travels at an incredible speed of:

**About 7.7 km per second**

At this speed, it completes one orbit around the Earth in roughly **90 minutes**.

This altitude is carefully chosen:

- Too high → increased radiation exposure (Van Allen belts)
- Too low → atmospheric drag would pull it down

To maintain its orbit, the ISS performs periodic **reboost maneuvers**.

## 🛰️ How Close Is It, Really?

For comparison:

👉 Geostationary satellites: ~36,000 km  
👉 GPS satellites: ~20,200 km

The ISS, by contrast, is only:

👉 **~400 km above Earth**

This huge difference explains why the ISS can sometimes be seen with the naked eye 🌏

## 📡 The ISS Transmits Radio Signals

In addition to dedicated communication systems, the ISS also operates on **amateur radio frequencies**.

### 📡 ISS Amateur Radio Frequencies

- Downlink (receive): **145.800 MHz (FM)**
- Uplink (transmit): **144.490 MHz (FM)**

Other operations include:

- Digipeater: 145.825 MHz
- SSTV (image transmission): 145.800 MHz
- UHF band: 437.800 MHz (used in some cases)

---

## ⚡ The Challenge: Doppler Shift

The ISS moves at about **7.7 km/s**, which causes a shift in the received frequency.

👉 This is known as the **Doppler shift**

In practice, this means you need to adjust:

👉 **145.800 MHz ±5 to 10 kHz**

to properly receive signals during a pass.
